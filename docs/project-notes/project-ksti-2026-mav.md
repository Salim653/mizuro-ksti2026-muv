Salim is building an entry for **Mini Autonomous Vehicle** at Robotics Competition at KSTI 2026 (ITS Surabaya, offline **12 November 2026**, Technical Meeting **8 November 2026**), under the MIZURO archive at `!! MIZURO/!!!!!!!!KSTI 2026`. Work started 2026-10-16.

**Hardware already in hand:** a Jetson Orin Nano, two IMX219-160 CSI modules, one 3S 5000 mAh LiPo. Everything else — frame, motor, ESC, servo, body MCU, power conversion, storage, second pack — is unbought.

**The budget reversal (this is the load-bearing constraint).** The first plan was *buy a stock 1:10 2WD Ackerman car* (its open rear diff already satisfies the "differential drive system" rule, and Ackerman steering earns STP = 0.25, a ~20–25% score multiplier). He then capped the frame at **Rp200k for the team**, which kills that plan — so the decision moved to **cut fiberglass / epoxy-glass ("termo", G-10) plates in a side-plate frame architecture, plus running gear harvested from a broken donor 1:10 car**. He proposed the fiberglass route himself; it is his call, not a recommendation to re-litigate.

**Why side-plate over touring flat-plate:** every part is a flat rectangle (cut-and-drill, no machined pockets), the closed rectangle is torsion-stiff, the drivetrain hangs *between* the rails so the battery sits low, and track/wheelbase become hole patterns. A touring graphite plate's whole value is *tuned flex* on asphalt at speed, which is useless in a 50 cm lane at 1 m/s. The real constraint on any 1:10 chassis is **payload: ~250–350 g before the CG rolls**, and the Jetson + carrier + SSD + fan is already 200–280 g.

**Body MCU decision: STM32F401/F411 Blackpill**, ~5–10 s rebuilds on the Arduino-stm32 core or sub-second with `stc-diy` + `make`; runner-up Raspberry Pi Pico 2. **ESP32 explicitly rejected** for rebuild latency (see [[feedback-rebuild-loop-latency]]) and because WiFi is banned during a run anyway. Firmware scope kept tiny: 330 Hz PWM ×2, 1 kHz tick, encoder capture, IMU over I²C, ~10-byte serial protocol, and a watchdog that zeroes both outputs if no setpoint arrives in 100 ms. The Jetson's GPIO has no hardware PWM, so an MCU is mandatory, not optional.

**Why (scoring):** reliability dominates speed — a missed checkpoint costs roughly twice what 30 s of saved lap time earns, and the run format is brutal (10 min prep, 5 min run, 3 attempts, no touching after start, **all comms banned during a run**, so no RC fallback and no mid-lap tuning). The IR/line-follower sensor ban is what forces the vision route at all.

**How to apply:** lead with the throttle-resolution trap, not exotic perception — a 1:10 at 1 m/s runs at 1–3% of full throttle, inside the ESC deadband, so gearing down plus clamping the pulse window in firmware beats any algorithm. Lane keeping needs **no depth sensing** (≥1 cm marks resolve to ~11 px from a nadir camera); camera questions should be answered as "forward-tilted for obstacles + nadir for centring," not stereo. Fiberglass failure modes to respect: it splits radially from holes (backing plates/washers, never self-tap, hole edge ≥1.5× diameter from a cut edge), cross-members are what stop two rails racking, and a rigid FRP mast transmits floor vibration into the lane-edge fit so the camera needs rubber isolation.

**Drivetrain decomposition (settled 2026-10-17).** Current plan: **own the frame design, own the wheels + steering-with-servo, but BUY the differential** — do not 3D-print one. An FDM bevel gear diff binds-when-tight / strips-when-loose and can't be backlash-tuned without reprinting, so printing is false economy next to a drop-in unit; the only print-safe diff is a locked **spool** (fine only if the track has no tight corners) — dual-motor + firmware speed-mixing was floated as the alternative that removes the diff entirely (his STM32 strength) but is not the chosen path. He is on the HSP 94111 family, whose **gear diff is part 02024 — but 02024 listings VARY: the one Salim found is a complete diff (metal gears in a plastic case, self-contained, no separate housing needed); other sellers ship it as gears-only. Always read the listing photo.** The diff/gearbox/12 mm hex/CVD are shared across the whole HSP 1:10 platform (94xxx models) plus the Redcat Volcano / Himoto rebadges — see [[reference-rc-chassis-sourcing]]. The wheel↔diff interface he must design to is **12 mm across-flats hex + M4 hub nut + CVD driveshaft** (HSP/Tamiya standard); his custom wheels need a true press-fit 12 mm hex socket (or a metal hex adapter pressed into a round bore) or the wheel cam-outs and spins. Steering is cheap to *buy* (metal knuckle ~Rp80k, part 02015) — he is making only the linkage + servo mount custom, not inventing Ackermann geometry.

**COMP RULE MANDATES AN OPEN DIFFERENTIAL (settled 2026-10-17 — load-bearing).** The KSTI ruling *requires* a mechanical open diff, so the **dual-motor + firmware speed-mixing (e-diff) route is DISQUALIFIED by definition** — there is no diff in it. Salim's own reasoning, which is correct: an open rear diff only makes sense with **Ackermann front steering** (it lets the two driven wheels roll at different speeds through a steered corner); banning e-diff + requiring an open diff is the organizers forcing the conventional car topology and blocking the trivial two-motor pivot build. **Therefore the architecture is locked: one motor → rear open diff → two driven wheels, PLUS Ackermann front steering/servo.** The **e-diff / dual-motor route is banned by the rule**, but a **SINGLE geared DC motor (JGA25/JGB37 class) coupled to the rear diff is back on the table** (validated 2026-10-17) — the ban was only on *two* motors that remove the diff; one geared motor + the open diff is rule-compliant, and the coupler-direct route uses exactly that. So the two live drivetrain options are **RC 540 + stock centre gearbox + rear prop + rear diff** (zero fabrication) or **geared DC motor + flexible coupler + adapter → rear diff** (shorter, needs an adapter, NOT a stepper). Verify at the Technical Meeting whether the rule says "open" specifically (then run the diff DRY = true open) or just "a differential" (then oil/lock is allowed).

**Drivetrain layout (settled 2026-10-17): rear-wheel drive, ONE diff, no AWD.** Buy one rear open gear diff (02024-type). Correction to an earlier note here: on the 94111 **both** axle diffs are **prop-driven bevel final drives** — the spur/pinion reduction lives in a separate **centre gearbox**, NOT on the rear diff (the earlier "rear drive-end has a spur mount / front is a yoke" framing was wrong; see [[reference-rc-chassis-sourcing]]). Front becomes **steering-only dead wheels on 12 mm hex + bearings** (decouples steering from drive, keeps weight over the driven axle, mild predictable understeer, one diff + two CVDs instead of a 4WD transfer case). Two feed routes: **(A)** keep the stock centre gearbox + rear propshaft + rear diff, delete only the front diff + front prop (zero fabrication); **(B)** coupler-direct geared DC motor → diff bevel input (needs a small adapter). Order pieces: rear gear diff (×1) + CVD `as roda` (×2, rear length) + 12 mm hex/M4 interface; spur/pinion only if going route A.

**Motor + speed ceiling (CORRECTED 2026-10-17 — the earlier "perception-latency-limited ~1.5–2 m/s" framing was wrong).** The arena-3d **20 fps is just an export sampling choice in the code, NOT the Jetson's real capture/inference rate** — Salim flagged this. The 50–100 Hz inner loop is not the limiter either. Once perception runs at the hardware rate (IMX219 720p60 + YOLO11n-seg @480 on the Orin Nano via TensorRT, end-to-end ~25–50 ms *estimate, measure it*), the real ceiling becomes **grip/geometry**: `v_corner = √(μ·g·R_min)` ≈ **2–2.5 m/s** for a tight arena corner at μ≈0.7 — the cap is set by the **tightest corner radius**, not fps or loop rate. Faster perception buys safety margin, not top speed. **Gear the motor for ~3 m/s top** (headroom above the corner-limited cruise), NOT down to 1.5 m/s. At 2 m/s on ~65 mm wheels ≈ 590 wheel RPM; through a ~5–7:1 reduction the motor runs ~3–4 k RPM and wheel torque is trivial (~25–35 mN·m) — **RPM-bound, not torque-bound**, don't oversize. **Diff-input sizing (2026-10-17):** the diff carrier/ring-gear input turns at **wheel speed** (carrier = average of the two axle speeds), so `diff input RPM = v/(π·D)·60` ≈ **588 RPM @2 m/s / 881 @3 m/s on a 65 mm wheel**, ~424/637 on a 90 mm wheel; motor RPM = diff input × (spur/pinion teeth), e.g. 64T/17T ≈ 3.76× → ~3.3 k RPM. **1:10 wheel Ø by class:** touring 64–70 mm (right target for a flat-arena bot — low CG), buggy 85–95 mm, **HSP 94111 monster donor tires 90–100 mm**. Pick wheel Ø first, then size the pinion so the motor lands ~3–5 k RPM at top speed. **Chosen pick unchanged: HSP 540/390 brushed + its ESC, open-loop forward speed** — stability comes from closed-loop *steering* (servo @ ~100 Hz), not regulated speed. The two numbers that actually set the ceiling, both to measure: (a) end-to-end perception ms on the Jetson, (b) the KSTI track's tightest corner radius `R_min`. **Drivetrain topology CORRECTED 2026-10-17 by reading the exploded view (page 30): the 94111 is a shaft-drive 4WD with a SEPARATE CENTRE GEARBOX + propshafts + bevel final drives + axle diffs = TWO reduction stages — the earlier "spur-driven single-stage diff, 64T = diff carrier" claim was wrong.** **3S motor target (corrected):** on the **coupler route (B)** the diff's bevel (~3.8:1, confirm teeth) is the only reduction, so target a **geared-DC-motor output ~2,400–3,400 RPM** (65 mm @3 m/s ≈ 3,350; 90 mm @3 ≈ 2,400). On the **stock chain (A)** total reduction ≈ ~14× so the 540 is heavily over-geared — **ignore KV, PWM-cap the ESC ~25–40%**. A bare 1200 KV drone outrunner is far too fast for either. Full spec in [[reference-rc-chassis-sourcing]].

**Suspension:** likely **skipped** (rigid mount) for a smooth flat indoor track at autonomous speeds — traction comes from tire grip, low CG, weight over drive wheels and hand-set toe/camber, not dampers. The one fact that decides this is the **KSTI 2026 track surface** (smooth floor vs carpet vs obstacles), still unconfirmed.
